Practice

Not the representation of a place, but the experience of looking.

Andrew Pengilly’s work considers how light, memory and the camera reshape our experience of place. It draws on nineteenth-century painting, the camera obscura and early photography to question the difference between what the observer sees and what the camera records.

Series are built as narratives rather than isolated images. The photographic book is central to the practice: sequence, scale, material and rhythm allow an impression of place to accumulate gradually, while meaning remains open.

“The work aims to convey an impression of place that can be felt by the spectator.”

Obscured, 2012

Profile

Photography shaped by a lifetime in visual culture.

Andrew Pengilly is an artist, photographer and creative director whose practice brings together image-making, editorial design and the artist’s book.

After studying Graphic Design at the University of Brighton, he worked with Alan Fletcher at Pentagram and later held creative director roles in London and Paris. He founded A2 Design in 2000 and Format Editions in 2013, combining his experience in design, print and binding with a renewed photographic practice.
